Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Film Tool Hub (filmtoolhub.com) is a set of free filmmaking tools. Your work stays in your own browser and, if you sign in, in your own Google Drive. We don't run a server or a database, and we never receive your work.

The short version
  • Your projects are saved in your browser, and in a Film Tool Hub folder in your own Google Drive if you sign in.
  • The site only asks Google for the drive.file permission. That means it can only see the files it creates in your Drive (and a project file someone shares with you, once you pick it). It can't see anything else you keep there.
  • The Casting Tracker can hold actors' contact details. These stay in your browser and your own Drive, like the rest of your work.
  • No adverts, no analytics, no tracking cookies, and nothing is sold. A project is only shared with other people if its owner chooses to share it (see “Sharing a project with your crew”).
  • You can delete everything yourself at any time (see “Removing your information”).

Who runs Film Tool Hub

Film Tool Hub is run by Timothy McGlinchey. For any question about this policy or your information, email tmcglinchey@belfastmet.ac.uk.

What you can use without signing in

Every tool works without an account. Whatever you make is saved in your web browser on that computer, using the browser's own storage. It isn't sent to us. Anyone else who uses the same browser profile on that computer may be able to see it.

Signing in with Google

Signing in is optional. It lets you keep your projects in your own Google Drive and carry on from any computer. When you sign in, Google asks you to allow two things:

WhatWhy
Your name, email address and profile pictureTo show who's signed in, and to keep your projects separate from anyone else who uses the same computer. This is kept in your browser only.
Access to the files Film Tool Hub creates in your Google Drive (the drive.file permission)To save your projects and their backups in a Film Tool Hub folder in your Drive, and to open them again. It also covers a project file someone has shared with you, once you choose that file when Google asks. The site can't see, read or change any other file in your Drive.

Your project files travel directly between your browser and your Google Drive. They don't pass through any server of ours, and we can't see them.

Film Tool Hub's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We don't use this information for advertising, we don't sell it or share it with anyone, and no person reads it.

Sharing a project with your crew

Sharing is optional and only the project's owner can do it. When you share a project, you type in your crew's email addresses and Google Drive gives those people access to that one project file, in the same way as sharing any file in Drive. Google emails each person a link.

  • Who can see what: everyone you share with can open and change everything in that project, including anything personal in it. For example, the Casting Tracker holds actors' names, contact details and photos. Only share with people who need to see it, and remove people when they leave the production (Share, then Remove).
  • What your crew see about you: your name and email address, so they can tell who is editing a tool and who saved last. This is stored in the shared project file in the owner's Google Drive.
  • Documentation: the documents saved in a shared project's Documentation (exports and drafts) are shared with everyone on it too, along with the name of whoever saved each one. Some may hold personal details, such as a casting contact sheet or a call sheet with phone numbers.
  • The crew list: the owner can give each person a name and a role. Everyone on the project sees that list (names, roles and email addresses), and tools use it to suggest names, for example when a task is given to someone.
  • Conversations: comments written in a shared project can be read by everyone on it, with the name and email address of whoever wrote each one and who it was for. They are kept in the shared project file.
  • Where it's kept: the shared file stays in the owner's Google Drive. Each person also has a working copy in their own browser. We still never receive any of it.
  • Stopping: the owner can remove a person at any time. That person keeps no access to the file, but a copy may remain in their browser until they remove it.

The Casting Tracker and actors' details

The Casting Tracker is for keeping track of the actors you audition. Whoever uses it types in the details, so it can hold information about other people, not just you. Depending on what you fill in, that can include:

  • actors' names, pronouns, where they're based, and their phone numbers and email addresses;
  • their agent's name, agency, phone number and email address;
  • headshots and photos, and links to self-tapes, showreels and profiles (for example Spotlight, Vimeo, YouTube or Instagram);
  • casting details such as playing age, height, build, look, gender, ethnicity, accents, skills, whether they're comfortable with stunts or intimate scenes, their availability and fee;
  • audition dates, notes and decisions.

All of this is kept in the same way as the rest of your work: in your browser, and in your own Google Drive if you sign in. We never receive it. If you share the project, everyone on it can see it (see “Sharing a project with your crew”).

If you use the Casting Tracker, you're responsible for the actors' information you put in it. Only record what you need for casting, tell actors how you'll keep their details, and delete them when the production no longer needs them. Some details, such as ethnicity, are sensitive, so only record them if the role genuinely needs it and the actor has agreed.

Voice notes

On the Ideas page you can tap the microphone and say your idea instead of typing it. The browser asks your permission before the microphone is used, and it's only on while the countdown is running (one minute at most). The recording is kept in your browser on that device; we never receive it.

While you speak, the site asks your browser to turn your words into text, so the idea can be read and searched later. Some browsers do this on your device, but others send the sound to their own speech service: Google Chrome, for example, sends it to Google, and Safari to Apple. Their privacy policies apply to that. If you'd rather it didn't happen, type your idea instead.

Instagram and other links

Film Tool Hub doesn't embed Instagram posts or feeds, and doesn't load anything from Instagram, Facebook or other social networks. In the Casting Tracker you can save a link, such as an actor's Instagram profile or a showreel, as plain text. Nothing is fetched from that link unless you click it, and it then opens in a new tab on that site, where that site's own privacy policy applies. The Pre-Production Pipeline mentions Instagram only as advice about promoting your film.

Services the site relies on

Like most websites, Film Tool Hub loads a few things from other companies. Each can see the basic technical details any website sees, such as your IP address and browser type, but not your work.

  • GitHub Pages hosts the website, and the filmtoolhub.com address is registered through Cloudflare. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Google provides sign-in, Google Drive, the file chooser used to open a shared project (Google Picker) and the website's fonts (Google Fonts). See Google's privacy policy.
  • cdnjs (Cloudflare) supplies some tools' building blocks, such as the code that reads PDFs and makes Excel files. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Cookies

Film Tool Hub doesn't use cookies for tracking or advertising. It uses your browser's storage to keep your work and settings, which is needed for the tools to work. Google may use its own cookies while you sign in.

Removing your information

  • From a computer: click your initial (top right), then Sign out, then Sign out and remove. You can also clear this site's data in your browser settings.
  • From your Google Drive: delete the Film Tool Hub folder.
  • A single saved document: open Documentation, open its folder and press Delete. It goes to your Google Drive's bin, which Google empties after 30 days; a copy is also kept in your browser for 30 days, in case you restore a backup.
  • A project shared with you: open Manage projects and choose Remove from my list. To be taken off it completely, ask the owner to remove you.
  • Stop the site using your Google account: go to your Google Account's third-party connections and remove Film Tool Hub.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have rights over your personal information, including to see it, correct it and delete it. Because your information stays in your own browser and Google Drive, you can do all of this yourself using the steps above. If you need help, or have a concern, email us. You can also contact the Information Commissioner's Office.

Changes to this policy

If the way Film Tool Hub handles information changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top.
