Colour temperature is the light

Measured in Kelvin. Lower numbers are warmer (candlelight, tungsten), higher numbers are cooler (shade, overcast sky). Move the top arrow to change the light.

White balance is the camera

It tells the camera which colour of light to treat as white. Wherever you set it, pure white moves with it. When it sits under the light's arrow, the colour is cancelled out and whites come out white.

Why 1000K isn't always 1000K

We see colour shifts in mireds (1,000,000 ÷ K), not Kelvin. 1000K under 5600K is a 39 mired shift, but 1000K over is only 27. That's why warm mismatches look stronger than cool ones.

Colours follow the blackbody curve. White balance is applied as a Bradford chromatic adaptation, with balanced white shown as display white.
